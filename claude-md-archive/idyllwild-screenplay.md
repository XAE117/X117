# IDYLLWILD

## Project Overview

Cosmic horror novelette. 17-scene structure. 8 of 17 scenes drafted (~10,800 words). Target: 13,000–16,000 words (novelette). First-person present tense, narrator Dougie. No quotation marks. Paratactic syntax. Trust the image, kill the commentary.

## Current State — Feb 16, 2026

**Completion: ~65-70%** of narrative weight. Scenes 1-8 drafted and revised through "Sparsity Draft" pass (Feb 4). Scene 8 (The Confession) drafted Feb 5. Full supporting architecture complete: 17-scene outline, Writers Room Bible (4 voice registers), Character Notes (behavioral maps), Editorial Panel Review (5 voices).

**What's left:** Scenes 9-17 (~3,000-4,500 words). These scenes accelerate — shorter, sharper, violence cascade into aftermath. Estimated 4-5 focused sessions to complete.

**Next scene:** Scene 9 — The Couch / First Violence (Peyton kills Braxton). ~300-500 words. This is where the story crosses over.

## Voice Registers (Dougie)

**Literary** (Scenes 1-4): Long sentences, precise observation, funny. Youth-brain associative drift.

**Sharpening** (Scenes 5-8): Shorter, sharper, uncomfortable precision. Drug taking effect.

**Breaking** (Scenes 9-14): Fragments, sensory overload, grammar fracturing. Extreme polysyndeton.

**Dead** (Scenes 15-17): Calm but wrong. Observational without affect. Floor-level, unblinking.

## Key Rules

- No quotation marks — dialogue sits flat in environment
- Trust the image, kill the commentary — no Dougie interpreting his own experience
- Marshall is peripheral vision — glimpses, not study
- Entities are agricultural workers — indifferent, not malevolent
- Violence is functional, not spectacular — describe and move on
- All violence is human-generated — entities never harm anyone
## Publication Targets

Electric Literature, [Tor.com](http://tor.com/), Nightmare Magazine. Self-publication preserves rights for AI film adaptation.

# The 7 Rules

  1. First person present tense. Always. No exceptions.
  1. Dougie narrates everything — his perception IS reality
  1. All violence is human. The entities don't hurt anyone directly.
  1. No exposition dumps. World-building through sensory detail only.
  1. The dose doesn't create the horror — it reveals what was already there
  1. Each voice register shift must be earned by story events
  1. The reader should be uncertain what's real by Scene 10
