# Full Pitch

A film processing childhood. Not a memoir — a reckoning. The title riffs on Harper Lee but the story is personal, specific, and unflinching.

The structure mirrors memory itself: non-linear, emotionally organized rather than chronologically. Scenes from childhood are presented not in the order they happened but in the order they matter — the way a mind returns to formative moments, circling them, recontextualizing them with adult understanding.

The film would use mixed media — home video footage (real), AI-generated recreations of memories that were never filmed, and present-day sequences of the filmmaker returning to physical locations and finding them changed. The gap between what happened, what was remembered, and what remains is the entire film.

This is autobiographical filmmaking as exorcism. The act of making the film IS the processing. The audience watches someone transform private pain into public art in real time.
