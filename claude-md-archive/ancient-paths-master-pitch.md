# Strategic Axes — How the Traditions Interconnect

## 1. The Ego-Dissolution Axis (Zen + Sufism + Advaita)

These three traditions act as a "hard reset" for the self-centeredness of the addict. While Zen uses paradox to break the mind, Advaita uses logic to see through the illusion of the "I," and Sufism uses the fire of love to burn the ego away. They all converge on the 12-Step goal of **self-forgetting.**

## 2. The Action-in-Nature Axis (Taoism + Red Road + Stoicism)

These traditions provide the "grounding" required for long-term sobriety. Taoism teaches how to move with the flow of life; the Red Road connects that flow to the Earth and ancestors; Stoicism teaches us to accept the flow we cannot change.

## 3. The Reconstruction Axis (Kabbalah + Christianity + Heathenry)

Once the "old life" is cleared, these paths offer frameworks for building a "New Design for Living." They focus on ethics, amends, and the ongoing labor of spiritual maintenance — turning a "shattered vessel" into a functional one.

---

# Neurodivergence Integration

**Structure vs. Flow:** Stoicism and Heathenry provide the "rules" and logic often needed by those with ASD/ADHD for executive function, while Taoism and Zen provide the "unmasking" and release from the pressure of perfectionism.

**Sensory Integration:** The ritualistic elements of the Red Road and Kabbalah (meditation on the Sefirot) offer somatic, non-verbal pathways to spirituality for those who find standard talk-therapy/AA meetings cognitively draining.
