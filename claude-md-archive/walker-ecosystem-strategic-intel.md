# Section 1: The Veteran-Recovery-AI Nexus

## The Crisis in Numbers

2.8 million veterans (14%) reported SUD in the prior year. Among post-9/11 veterans, probable alcohol use disorder is double that of pre-9/11 veterans (8% vs 4%). Yet 96% of veterans with SUD did not believe they needed treatment, and only 28–30% receive any care in a given year. A 2021 study of 40,909 veteran treatment episodes found an approximately 94% relapse rate upon discharge.

The VA OIG documented 2–3 month waitlists at VA North Texas, staff misinterpreting the MISSION Act. VHA estimated 3,500 patients nationally pending admission with average waits exceeding 150 days. A 2026 interim final rule tying disability ratings to medicated symptoms has veterans potentially avoiding SUD medications to protect compensation.

More than 20% of veterans with PTSD also have SUD, and 63% of recent veterans meeting SUD criteria had co-occurring PTSD — yet treatment programs remain structurally separated at most facilities.

## The Moral Injury Gap

Over 90% of 373 veterans surveyed reported high-severity moral injury symptoms (9-10 on a 1-10 scale). Moral injury is not a DSM diagnosis, has no standardized treatment pathway, and remains an independent risk factor for depression, anxiety, and suicide even after controlling for PTSD. Only a handful of experimental interventions exist: Adaptive Disclosure Therapy (Brett Litz), Building Spiritual Strength (8-week spiritually integrated group), and Structured Pastoral Care (12-session chaplain intervention).

**No nationally standardized veteran-specific recovery curriculum exists.** Veteran-only AA/NA meetings use standard 12-step curriculum without addressing moral injury, operational stress, identity loss, or hypervigilance.
