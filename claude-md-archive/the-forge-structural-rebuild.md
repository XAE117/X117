# I. The Protocol

## The Daily Morning Primer (15 Minutes)

Do this *before* you sit down to edit or compose. It wakes up the stabilizers that have gone dormant. This is your non-negotiable — like the Morning Inventory, but for the body. If your energy is 1-2 and you can only do one physical thing today, do this.

**McGill Big Three (3 Rounds):**

Modified Curl-Up: 6 reps with 10-second holds. Hands under the low back for support. One knee bent, one straight. Lift head and shoulders only — this is NOT a sit-up. The movement is tiny and the hold is where the work happens.

Side Plank (from knees if needed): 6 reps with 10-second holds per side. Stack your elbow under your shoulder. The goal is to create a rigid line from knees to head. If this triggers the back weakness, regress to a wall lean.

Bird-Dog: 6 reps with 10-second holds per side. On all fours, extend opposite arm and leg simultaneously. The key is keeping the hips perfectly level — imagine balancing a glass of water on your lower back. Slow and controlled.

**Glute Bridge Iso-Hold:** Lie on your back, knees bent, feet flat. Lift hips and hold for 30 seconds. Focus entirely on the glutes firing — if you feel it in the lower back, you've gone too high. The glutes are the engine that protects your spine.

**Dead Bug:** 10 slow, controlled reps. On your back, arms extended toward ceiling, knees at 90 degrees. Slowly lower opposite arm and leg toward the floor. Your lower back must stay "glued" to the floor the entire time. If it arches, you've gone too far.

**Total time:** 12-15 minutes. Non-negotiable.

## The "Best Body" Resistance Routine (3x Weekly)

**Status: PENDING — Requires doctor clearance before starting.**

Focus on Time Under Tension rather than heavy loads. Slow, controlled movements. The goal is structural integrity, not ego lifting.

**Chest-Supported Row** — 3 sets of 12 reps. Builds thick upper back without loading the spine. Lie face-down on an incline bench and row dumbbells up. The bench takes all the spinal compression out of the movement.

**Floor Press** — 3 sets of 10 reps. Chest and tricep development while the floor acts as a natural brace for your back. Like a bench press, but lying on the floor limits the range of motion and eliminates the arch that can aggravate the spine.

**Spanish Squats** — 3 sets of 15 reps. Loop a resistance band behind the knees and anchor it to something solid. Sit back into the squat — the band shifts the load off the spine and onto the quads. Leg drive without the crushing weight of a barbell.

**Wall Sits** — 3 sets of 45-second holds. Back flat against the wall, thighs parallel to the floor. Builds quad endurance with zero spinal movement. Simple, brutal, effective.

**Progression Plan:** Start with bodyweight or light dumbbells. Add weight only when form is perfect at current load for all sets. This is a 6-month timeline — there is no rush.

# II. Lifestyle Architecture

## The 20/2 Rule

For every 20 minutes in the editing chair, 2 minutes in a decompression state. This isn't just standing — it's one of these:

The **Psoas Stretch**: Half-kneeling lunge, rear knee on the floor, front foot forward. Push hips gently forward. The psoas is the deep hip flexor that tightens during sitting and pulls directly on the lumbar spine.

The **90/90 Position**: Lie on the floor, legs up on a chair so both hips and knees are at 90 degrees. This is the reset position. Let gravity decompress the spine. 2 minutes here after 20 minutes of sitting.

**Implementation:** Set a recurring timer on your phone or watch. Every 20 minutes it fires and you move. No negotiation, no "one more cut." The editing chair is a slow-motion injury if unmanaged.

## The Director's Walk (3x Daily)

Three 15-minute walks per day. Walking is nature's chiropractor — the reciprocal movement of arms and legs creates a gentle pumping action for the spinal discs that nothing else replicates.

Your former ultra-runner engine is the asset here. You're not training for speed or distance. You're using the most natural human movement to keep the spine healthy while building toward Whitney.

Start with one walk tomorrow. Build to two by week 2, three by week 3. Anchor them: morning walk before work, midday walk between sessions, evening walk as a wind-down.

## Sleep & Recovery

Your sobriety is a structural advantage here. Without alcohol, your systemic inflammation is already lower than most people's baseline. Capitalize on this:

Prioritize 8 hours of sleep. The "best body" is built during the repair phase, especially with a bone-related injury. Compression fractures heal during rest, not during activity.

Hydration target: Half your body weight in ounces, daily. The intervertebral discs are largely water — dehydration makes them compress faster under load.

# IV. The Environment

## Studio Ergonomics

Your composing and editing station is where you spend the most consecutive hours. Setup checklist:

Monitor at eye level — top of screen at or slightly below eye line. No looking down at a laptop screen for hours. If using a laptop, get an external monitor or a laptop stand with a separate keyboard.

Chair height: feet flat on floor, thighs parallel to ground, 90-degree angle at the knees. If your chair doesn't support this, a footrest fixes it.

Lumbar support: a small pillow or rolled towel in the curve of your lower back. This maintains the natural lordotic curve and prevents the "slumping" that loads the compression fracture site.

Keyboard and mouse close to the body — no reaching. Reaching forward pulls the shoulders and rounds the thoracic spine, which cascades down to the lumbar.

Physical reminder: Sticky note on the monitor that says **"20/2"** so you never forget the decompression rule.

## Travel Protocol (Medford & Beyond)

Whether driving or flying, the compression fracture makes prolonged sitting dangerous.

**Lumbar roll:** A rolled-up towel in the small of the back maintains the natural curve. Don't travel without it.

**Iso-tension during travel:** Every 30 minutes while seated, perform 10 seated glute squeezes and 10 seconds of seated core bracing (push belly button toward spine, hold, release). This keeps the stabilizers from going completely slack during long sits.

**Stops:** If driving, stop every 60 minutes minimum. Walk for 5 minutes. Do the psoas stretch at the car. Your back will thank you for the next 60 miles.

**Post-travel:** When you arrive, do the full Morning Primer before anything else. Travel compresses the discs — the Primer re-engages the muscular corset.

# VI. Red Flags — When to Stop

**Sharp, electric shock sensation:** Full stop. This means a nerve is being compressed. Go to 90/90 position immediately. If it persists beyond 10 minutes, call the doctor.

**Sudden numbness in legs:** Full stop. This is the barrier being breached. 90/90 position, gentle breathing, no movement until sensation returns.

**Radiating pain down one leg:** This suggests disc involvement beyond the compression fracture. Do not push through it. Log it in the Barrier Map and report to your doctor.

**Increasing weakness over days:** If the episodes are getting more frequent or more severe, something is changing. Doctor visit is urgent, not elective.

**The core principle:** We are building a body that is functional and pain-free. This year is about moving from "endurance athlete" to "structural powerhouse." Every session where you don't get hurt is a win. Every day you do the Primer is a deposit in the bank that makes Whitney possible.

# I. The Protocol

## OMAD — One Meal A Day (Daily Default)

  **Eating Window:** 3:00 PM – 5:00 PM

  **Fasting Window:** 5:00 PM – 3:00 PM next day (22 hours)

  During your window, eat one substantial meal. This isn't restriction — it's consolidation. You're compressing your daily nutrition into a focused window so your body spends most of its time in a fasted, fat-burning state.

  **Meal Composition Target:**

  - Protein: 40-50% of plate (150g+ daily target) — chicken, fish, eggs, beef, Greek yogurt
  - Healthy fats: 25-30% — avocado, olive oil, raw nuts (your existing snack swap)
  - Complex carbs: 20-25% — sweet potato, rice, quinoa, vegetables
  - Minimize: refined carbs, sugar, processed food, liquid calories
  **The 218→200 Math:**

  - 18 lbs to lose
  - At 1.5 lbs/week = 12 weeks (target: ~April 29)
  - At 2 lbs/week = 9 weeks (target: ~April 8)
  - The 48-hour fasts accelerate this significantly
## Weekly 48-Hour Fast

  **Schedule:** Sunday 5 PM → Tuesday 5 PM (or adjust based on your week)

  This is your proven protocol. One full 48-hour fast per week creates a significant weekly caloric deficit and gives your system a deep reset.

  **During the 48:**

  - Water (unlimited)
  - Black coffee (no cream, no sugar)
  - Electrolytes (sodium, potassium, magnesium — critical for avoiding headaches and fatigue)
  - Herbal tea
  - Nothing else
  **Breaking the fast:** Break with something gentle — bone broth, eggs, or a small portion of your regular meal. Don't break with a massive feast. Give your digestion 30 minutes before your full meal.

## The Rolling 48s (Aspirational Target)

  Once the weekly 48 feels effortless (typically 3-4 weeks in), you can experiment with rolling 48s: eat one OMAD meal, then fast 48 hours, eat one OMAD meal, fast 48 hours. This is the accelerated protocol. Don't force it — let the weekly 48s build your fasting muscle first.
